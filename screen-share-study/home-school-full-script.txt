WHO TAKES CARE? A COLORFUL STORY GAME
Complete Home / School study script reference
Current candidate: chs-home-school-evelyn-v1-r13

PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This is the complete researcher-facing script reference for the current Home / School candidate. It lists the fixed participant flow and every line that can vary by setting, event, relationship pairing, response order, or follow-up focal person.

One child receives:
- one setting for all six stories: At Home OR At School;
- one event for all six stories: Hug OR Food OR Help;
- one six-story role set: Woman, Man, OR Family / Teacher;
- one visual rendition family (A, B, C, or D); and
- one follow-up focal-person schedule.

IMPORTANT CURRENT-BUILD CHECK

The current r13 game asks five ratings after every story: love, like, in charge, old, and strong. It does not currently ask only love and in charge. The five traits appear in a seed-randomized order.

Each prerecorded context question has a 450 millisecond total pause at the comma, immediately before “at the kid's home” or “at the kid's school.”

The setting cue is repeated once inside each of the six stories, after the last character is introduced and before the event begins. It always refers to the kid's home or the kid's school, including when an adult is the person in the middle.

CHS PARTICIPANT FLOW

1. CHS camera configuration.
2. CHS guardian video consent.
3. Recording begins.
4. In-game grown-up welcome.
5. In-game quick checks.
6. In-game camera directions.
7. Grown-up-to-child handoff.
8. Child welcome.
9. Six interleaved story + follow-up sequences, in randomized story order.
10. Child completion and grown-up handoff.
11. Recording stops.
12. Standard CHS exit survey.
13. Custom study debrief.

The child-assent screen exists in the code but is currently disabled and is not shown. The separate “Let’s play” game-start line also exists in the code but is not shown in the current one-after-story flow.


PART A. IN-GAME GROWN-UP SETUP

Page 1 — Grown-up welcome

“Welcome, grown-ups! Thank you for helping your child take part. First, we'll get the sound, screen, and camera ready. Then your child will listen to stories and choose pictures on the screen. Most pages move on by themselves, and you can press Replay if your child wants to hear a page again. You can help with the device, but please let your child choose the answers.”

Page 2 — Quick checks

“Before you begin: This is a recorded picture game about social relationships. It takes about ten to fifteen minutes. You and your child may stop at any time. There are no right or wrong answers in this game. The pages are read aloud, so your child does not need to read. Most pages in the game move on by themselves after a few moments, but you can press Replay to hear it again or press Next to move on sooner when it appears. Now, three quick checks. Use one screen and place it in front of your child. Turn the sound to a comfortable volume. Stay close to help with the device, but let your child choose the answers.”

Page 3 — Camera directions

“Let's check the camera. Put the screen directly in front of your child. Keep their full face and shoulders in view, and avoid a bright window behind them. Use one screen, and keep the webcam centered above the screen your child is watching.”

Page 4 — Grown-up-to-child handoff

“Grown-up setup is finished. Now it's your child's turn. Please invite your child to sit in front of the screen. Grown-ups, you may help with the device, but please let your child choose. There are no right or wrong answers. The pages are read aloud. Most pages move on by themselves. Press Replay to hear it again, or Next to move on sooner. When your child is ready, press the green button to continue.”


PART B. CHILD WELCOME

“Hi there! Welcome to Who Takes Care? We are going to look at pictures and play a choosing game. Listen to each page. When you see choices, choose the one you pick. When you are ready, hit the green button to start.”


PART C. EACH OF THE SIX STORIES

The following sequence repeats six times. The six story pairings depend on the assigned role set and appear in a randomized order.

1. “Look! Here are some people.”

2. “This little one is a kid.”

3–4. The two candidate identity lines for the assigned pairing. See Part D. Renditions A and B introduce the first-named candidate and then the second-named candidate. Renditions C and D reverse those two identity lines.

5. Assigned setting cue, after the last candidate identity line:
   - Home: “They are all at the kid's home.”
   - School: “They are all at the kid's school.”

6. The assigned event line. See Part E.

7. The assigned context question followed by the two-choice line. See Parts D and E.

8. After the child selects a character:
   - “You chose the best friend!”
   - “You chose the brother!”
   - “You chose the classmate!”
   - “You chose the dad!”
   - “You chose the friend!”
   - “You chose the kid!”
   - “You chose the mom!”
   - “You chose the sister!”
   - “You chose the teacher!”

9. Follow-up transition:

“Now let's answer some questions about the [focal person] inside the box and the [other person].”

Teacher / Teacher wording: “Now let's answer some questions about the teacher inside the box and the other teacher.”

10. “First, we’ll meet the two people so it is clear who each question is about.”

11–13. Follow-up identity sequence:
   - “Look! Here are some people.”
   - identity line for the other person;
   - identity line for the focal person.

14–18. Five rating questions, in a seed-randomized order. See Part F.

PART E. SETTING / EVENT LANGUAGE

The recipient is the KID for every ordinary pairing.

Directional recipient exceptions:
- DAD – KID: MOM is in the middle.
- MOM – KID: DAD is in the middle.
- TEACHER – KID: TEACHER is in the middle.

HUG

Event:
“Oh no! The [recipient] in the middle is sad now!”

Home question:
“Who will give the [recipient] in the middle a hug now that they're sad, at the kid's home?”

School question:
“Who will give the [recipient] in the middle a hug now that they're sad, at the kid's school?”

FOOD

Home event:
“Oh no! At the kid's home, the [recipient] in the middle is hungry now!”

School event:
“Oh no! At the kid's school, the [recipient] in the middle is hungry now!”

Home question:
“Who will give the [recipient] in the middle strawberries now that they're hungry, at the kid's home?”

School question:
“Who will give the [recipient] in the middle strawberries now that they're hungry, at the kid's school?”

HELP

Home event:
“Oh no! At the kid's home, the [recipient] in the middle dropped a heavy box!”

School event:
“Oh no! At the kid's school, the [recipient] in the middle dropped a heavy box!”

Home question:
“Who will help the [recipient] in the middle pick up the heavy box, at the kid's home?”

School question:
“Who will help the [recipient] in the middle pick up the heavy box, at the kid's school?”


PART F. FOLLOW-UP RATINGS

The selected focal person varies by relationship and pairing schedule. Before the response choices, the screen displays:

“Questions are about the [focal person] inside the box.”

Teacher / Teacher version:

“Questions are about the teacher inside the box, not the other teacher.”

The five spoken question-and-scale pairs are:

LOVE
“How much does the [focal person] love the [other person]?”
“Does not love. Loves a little. Loves a lot.”

LIKE
“How much does the [focal person] like the [other person]?”
“Does not like. Likes a little. Likes a lot.”

IN CHARGE
“How much is the [focal person] in charge?”
“Not in charge. A little in charge. Very much in charge.”

OLD
“How old is the [focal person]?”
“Not old. A little old. Very old.”

STRONG
“How strong is the [focal person]?”
“Not strong. A little strong. Very strong.”

Possible focal / other-person directions in the current schedules:

- brother → kid

- best friend → kid

PROCEDURES

“Your participation is completely voluntary; you and your child can choose not to take part. You and your child can agree to take part and later change your mind. The study session will be conducted remotely and recorded through Children Helping Science, an online platform for developmental research studies. During the study, your child will play a picture game called Who Takes Care? The game includes colorful pictures and prerecorded narration. Your child will hear six stories that all take place either at the kid's home or at the kid's school. In each story, someone needs comfort, food, or help and two characters could respond. Your child will select who is more likely to help and then answer questions about the characters, including who is in charge and how much one character loves another. Children do not need to be able to read to participate. Children are randomly assigned to one version of the game; different versions include different locations, relationship sets, and events. The study takes about 15 minutes. Please keep the sound on and let the game audio play. A grown-up may help with the device, but please do not suggest answers or point to a choice. All responses are stored automatically and uploaded to the research team. The recording is used for research purposes so the team can check how children responded during the task. We do not believe there are any risks for your child from participating in this research. Participation is completely voluntary. You and your child can stop at any time without penalty or loss of benefits to which you are otherwise entitled.”

PURPOSE

“We invite your child to take part in a research study about how children connect caregiving with different kinds of social relationships and settings. Children notice social roles, relationships, and locations and use them to make predictions about what people will do. This study asks whether children's caregiving expectations differ when the same kinds of events take place at home versus at school. By comparing children's helper choices with their answers about authority and affection, we can learn how they connect caregiving with relationships and settings.”
