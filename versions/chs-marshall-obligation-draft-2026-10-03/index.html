Draft decisions & sources

This is a review adaptation of Marshall et al. (2022), Study 1, using all three existing Find the Caregiver pairing sets. Each assigned set has six stories; the combined 18-story view is for review. Colors and character positions follow one existing balanced visual plan. The orange mom–sister story keeps mom on the left. Every story uses sadness, two helpers, and a plain background. Original study files are unchanged.

Predictions and obligations are asked independently for each helper. The story always continues with neither helping. A proposed “How sure are you?” confidence question follows each helper’s prediction after either Yes or No. There are ten main judgments per story (60 in a six-story session), including two new confidence responses, plus identity and recall checks, practice, and conditional strength questions. The original Find the Caregiver trait/Likert section is omitted.

Proposed wording: prediction confidence is a new adaptation measure, using “How sure are you?” and the provisional choices “Not sure,” “A little sure,” and “Very sure.” The confidence recordings are included; the wording and measure remain provisional. The overall “How mean was the thing that happened?” follow-up and its response options. The official supplement confirms an overall intensity measure and score levels, but the exact spoken prompt and responses were not recovered. It is flagged wherever shown.

Review choices: stories use a fixed order and helpers are asked from left to right for review; the published obligation order was randomized. Practice repeats in every story by default, as in the captured Marshall sequence. The optional first-story-only setting is a new adaptation. Practice strength follow-ups are shown after Yes; original practice routing remains unverified. Identity, recall and practice checks give a reminder after an incorrect answer and repeat until correct. There is no facilitator bypass; research exclusion rules are not implemented in this preview.

The “Now, sometimes people HAVE TO do things” recording uses continuous speech. Caption emphasis remains uppercase and italic.

Narration and answer choices play automatically. A speaker button replays them; there is no self-reading option. If the browser blocks the first automatic playback, tap the speaker once to start. Each spoken option glows as it is read, and responses unlock after narration finishes. Children tap the characters themselves for helper choices, then confirm with Yes or No. Practice gives an explanation after either answer and repeats incorrect checks until correct. No facilitator bypass is offered. Mean / Not Mean and rating boxes glow and gently hop as the options are spoken. Answers remain in memory during review; the CHS draft remains a private preview with participant response submission disabled.
