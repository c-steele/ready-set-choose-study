1. What we are studying

Previous research suggests that even infants and toddlers use clues about close relationships to anticipate who will respond when someone is upset. This study asks whether children’s expectations about who helps, comforts, or shares food also depend on whether a care need arises at home or at school. Children choose between two possible helpers in stories involving the same relationships in both settings. Comparing these choices helps us learn whether children consider where people are, as well as how they are related, when predicting who will provide care.

2. What your child did

Your child heard 12 stories about family members, friends, a teacher, and a classmate: six at the house and six at the school. In each story, your child chose which of two characters was more likely to help.

Some children see the house stories first, and others see the school stories first. Varying the order helps us distinguish differences related to the setting from differences caused by practice or tiredness.

3. How we interpret the answers

Children may make different choices for many reasons, and every answer is okay. We look at patterns across many children rather than judging any individual response.
