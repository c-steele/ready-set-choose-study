The full forced-choice session

Read in order. Each story has three helper comparisons, a rating of the selected meaner helper, individual evaluations of both helpers, and an explicit no-one-helped outcome. The grey notes describe the adaptation and comprehension checks.

Draft decisions & sources

This is a review adaptation of Marshall et al. (2022), Study 1, using all three existing Find the Caregiver pairing sets. Each assigned set has six stories; the combined 18-story view is for review. Colors and character positions follow one existing balanced visual plan. The orange mom–sister story keeps mom on the left. Every story uses sadness, two helpers, and a plain background. Original study files are unchanged.

Each story asks three main questions: “Who do you think will help…?”, “Who do you think HAS TO help…?”, and “Who was meaner for NOT helping…?” Children choose between the two helpers. The three comparisons are followed by a meanness strength rating for the chosen helper and Mean / Not Mean judgments about each helper, with a strength rating after Mean. A six-story session includes 30 main judgments, plus ratings, confirmations, introductions, comprehension checks and brief practice.

Adaptation: Marshall Study 1 asked individual prediction and obligation questions. This version measures relative helper choice. Choosing one helper does not establish that the other helper has no obligation. All stories continue with the same explicit outcome: no one helps.

Review choices: stories use a fixed review order; the two choices follow the helpers’ left and right positions in the scene. The brief HAVE TO practice runs once by default; the review control also allows repetition in every story. Checks give a spoken reminder and require the correct answer before continuing. This private preview does not implement research exclusion rules.

The “Now, sometimes people HAVE TO do things” recording uses continuous speech. Caption emphasis remains uppercase and italic.

Full narration script · Full recording index

Narration and answer choices play automatically. In child view, story-only screens advance after narration finishes; questions and character identification wait for a response. A speaker button replays narration and Pause stops it. There is no self-reading option or separate Let’s listen screen. If browser policy blocks the first audible recording, one tap anywhere on the story allows it to start. Each spoken option glows as it is read, and responses unlock after narration finishes. Children tap the characters themselves for helper choices, then confirm with Yes or No. Practice gives an explanation after either answer and repeats incorrect checks until correct. No facilitator bypass is offered. Mean / Not Mean and rating boxes glow and gently hop as the options are spoken. Answers remain in memory during review; the CHS draft remains a private preview with participant response submission disabled.
