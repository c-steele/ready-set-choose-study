Draft decisions & sources

This is a review adaptation of Marshall et al. (2022), Study 1, using all three existing Find the Caregiver pairing sets. Each assigned set has six stories; the combined 18-story view is for review. Colors and character positions follow one existing balanced visual plan. The orange mom–sister story keeps mom on the left. All six stories in a session share one type of care: giving a hug, sharing food, or helping pick up a heavy box. Original study files are unchanged.

Each story asks three questions about the two helpers. Children choose who will help and who has to help, in the same assigned order throughout the session. After learning that neither person helped, children choose who was meaner. The three comparisons are followed by a meanness strength rating for the chosen helper and a Mean / Not Mean judgment about only the other helper, with a strength rating after either answer. Both helpers receive the same four-option scale: Not mean, A teeny bit mean, A little bit mean, Very mean. The chosen helper is not rated again. Across six stories, children make 18 forced choices, rate both helpers in each story, and answer six recall checks.

Adaptation: Marshall Study 1 asked individual prediction and obligation questions. This version measures relative helper choice. Choosing one helper does not establish that the other helper has no obligation. All stories continue with the same explicit outcome: no one helps.

Review choices: each six-story session uses its own shuffled pairing order; the two choices follow the helpers’ left and right positions in the scene. If a child answers Yes to the recall check, the two nonhelping outcome screens replay, then the question is asked again. This repeats until No. This private preview does not implement research exclusion rules.

Script for this session · Full recording index

Narration and answer choices play automatically. In child view, story-only screens advance after narration finishes; questions wait for a response. A green Next arrow sits beside Replay on narration pages. Standalone previews can use it to move ahead immediately; embedded CHS sessions keep it locked until narration finishes. A speaker button replays narration and Pause stops it. There is no self-reading option or separate Let’s listen screen. If browser policy blocks the first audible recording, one tap anywhere on the story allows it to start. On helper-question screens, each character’s outline glows while its role name is read. Responses unlock after narration finishes. Children tap a character to record their choice and continue directly to the next part. No facilitator bypass is offered. The question outlines pulse in place without bouncing. Answers remain in memory during review; the CHS draft remains a private preview with participant response submission disabled.
