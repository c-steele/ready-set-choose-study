# Forced-choice obligation study draft

October 3, 2026. This is a separate compact version of the Marshall obligation follow-up. The individual-rating draft and the original Find the Caregiver / Who Helps Where studies are separate files and remain unchanged by this version.

Open `index.html` for the child preview or full storyboard. Pairing selectors show the Woman, Man, or Family set of six stories; All pairings shows 18 entries for researcher review. Each main question asks the child to choose between the two helpers.

| Main question | Example wording | Responses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prediction | Who do you think will help the kid in the middle? | The kid’s mom / The kid’s sister |
| Obligation | Who do you think HAS TO help the kid in the middle? | The kid’s mom / The kid’s sister |
| Meanness | Who was meaner for NOT helping the kid in the middle? | The kid’s mom / The kid’s sister |

These are forced comparisons between helpers. There is no both, neither, or equal option. Prediction and obligation are new forced-choice adaptations of the Marshall paradigm, rather than the original Study 1 individual Yes/No measures. The meaner comparison is adapted to the two-helper pairings and repeats the recipient reference.

## Story sequence

1. Meet the group.
2. Introduce the three characters cumulatively, with a tap on each introduced character. Preserve the source intro order, which begins with the kid even when the recipient is an adult.
3. Say the recipient is sad.
4. Say both helpers see that the recipient is sad.
5. Ask who will help. The two helper choices follow their positions in the scene, left then right.
6. Run brief HAVE TO comprehension practice: introduction, teacher/stop-being-mean Yes/No item, lie Yes/No item, and return to the story. Practice can repeat for every story or occur once.
7. Ask who HAS TO help, with the same two helper choices.
8. State the fixed outcome: no one helped, then name each helper who did NOT help.
9. Ask one Yes/No recall check: did either person help? The expected answer is No; a correction restates the explicit outcome when needed.
10. Ask who was meaner for NOT helping the recipient, with the same helper choices.
11. Continue to the next pairing, then thank the child at session end.

There are **three main judgments per story**, with no individual helper ratings, overall Mean/Nice judgment, individual recall questions, or how-much scales. The two practice items remain Yes/No comprehension checks and are not main dependent variables. Prediction or obligation responses never change whether the helpers help: the nonhelping outcome is fixed.

Each six-story version includes 18 character-identification taps, six recall checks, and either 12 or two practice selections. There are no conditional strength branches. These are script-based estimates using 110–130 words per minute and 3–5 seconds per selection, with each answer label read once. They are not observed child-session durations. Breaks, reminders, setup, transitions, and extra gaps between glowing options can add time. All 18 entries are a review catalog, not the proposed session length for a child.

## Stimuli and presentation

The pairing catalog, white backgrounds, exact existing characters and palettes, plain sadness context, original character positions, and cumulative character introductions come from the existing draft. Family-set stories with an adult recipient keep that adult in the middle. The two-teacher story distinguishes the helper as “the teacher on the left” and the recipient as “the teacher in the middle.” No extra family relationship is inferred.

Choices always match the two displayed helpers. Their full relationship descriptions are read and appear on buttons. Each option carries the matching actor ID so the reader can glow the button and corresponding character together. No school or home backdrop is added.

## Scripts and narration

The preview defaults to practice once. `SCRIPT.md` is the Woman set with practice once. `SCRIPT-{woman,man,family,all}-{each,once}.md` provides all eight combinations with every prompt, choice, check, and corrective reminder. The full storyboard can also be printed.

New forced-choice prompts need new NaturalReaders recordings. Existing recordings may be reused only when the complete spoken text matches exactly. A changed prompt must not play an older individual-rating recording. The narration manifest and recording index document current coverage and pending clips; duration remains illustrative until the required recordings are connected.

## Scope and source limits

This is a two-helper sadness adaptation using the relationship pairings from Find the Caregiver. It is not a verbatim copy of Marshall et al. (2022), Study 1, which used individual Yes/No prediction and obligation measures with follow-ups. Brief practice, explicit nonhelping narration, corrective reminders, fixed review order, and on-screen taps are implementation choices in this draft. Six stories per role set is retained for review; assignment and randomized presentation order are separate study-design decisions.

This preview is for reviewing the proposed task. Do not submit, activate, or recruit from this draft without the user’s separate instruction.
